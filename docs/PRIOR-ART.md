# Prior art: is YuDuino really new?

Jonah asked us to double-check that this product doesn't already exist. This is a time-boxed web search (about a dozen queries with Firecrawl, 2026-11-15) of products, papers and patents. **It is not a patent or freedom-to-operate search and not legal advice.** "We found none" means *we found none in this search*, not "none exists".

## The short answer

**Every ingredient already exists. We did not find the combination.** So the honest claim is **not** "a completely new technology". It is:

> *A new combination of known parts, aimed at a gap we could not find filled: an authenticator hidden inside an ordinary mouse that needs no input from the user, whose sensing is attested by hardware, that rejects other hands and detects coercion from the same passive stream, and whose approvals are post-quantum signed.*

Use that, or "to our knowledge, nothing combines these". Do **not** say "nothing like it exists" or "the first".

## What exists, and how close it is

| Area | What we found | How close | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mouse dynamics authentication** | A large academic literature on continuous authentication from how a person moves a mouse (for example [arXiv 2403.03828](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.03828), [Mondal and Bours](https://dl.gi.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/8442a562-b1ad-4437-8f8d-6f654c8c7c5c/content), a [survey](https://www.academia.edu/966533/A_survey_of_user_authentication_based_on_mouse_dynamics)); commercial vendors describe it ([TypingDNA](https://www.typingdna.com/glossary/what-is-mouse-dynamics-and-how-it-works)). Reported accuracy ranges from poor on a single action to low error rates when many actions are combined; results depend heavily on data and setup | **Very close** on the idea of "identify you by your mouse use, with no extra step" | **Not new.** Our owner check is in this family. Ours is different in that the sensing is **inside the mouse and attested by the STM32**, not cursor data read by software on the laptop that malware could observe or replay |
| **Continuous and implicit authentication products** | [BioCatch](https://www.biocatch.com/) and similar monitor behaviour through a session; a [survey of 140+ approaches](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08578) covers motion-based methods; [Nymi Band](https://www.nymi.com/nymi-band) is a wearable "always on" authenticator for enterprises | **Close** on "authenticate continuously and quietly" | **Not new.** Those are software on the endpoint, or a visible wearable. Ours is a hidden object that also gates a cryptographic signature |
| **Biometric mice** | Fingerprint and palm mice exist: [Cherry MC 4900](https://www.amazon.com/clp/B00JPDUJAY), [Lenovo Fingerprint Mouse](https://media.bechtle.com/asrc/180712/1c4b3d4ee288fc9434f5175bf56070570/c3/-/555a809d448a4dc48d317c22049459ae/len-usb-mouse-with-fingerprint-sensor-g3-datablad-1), [Fujitsu PalmSecure mouse](https://store.fulcrumbiometrics.com/products/palmsecure-f-pro-mouse-mouse-guide), [Thermaltake Black FP](https://www.wired.com/story/tt-esports-black-fp-biometric-mouse/) | **Close** on "a mouse used for authentication" | **Not new**, but they require a deliberate fingerprint or palm action and are visibly biometric. Ours has **no input** and nothing looks like an authenticator |
| **Mice with physiological sensors** | Research mice measure heart rate, skin conductance, skin temperature, hand humidity and grip force: [Androutsou et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9855736/), the [EMBC21 smart mouse](https://www.paperhost.org/proceedings/embs/EMBC21/files/1206.pdf), [CogniMouse](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/309763259_A_Computer_Mouse_for_Stress_Identification_of_Older_Adults_at_Work), a [Cornell project](https://people.ece.cornell.edu/land/courses/ece5030/FinalProjects/s2014/asn54_ebl43_hnl27/asn54_ebl43_hnl27/ECE%205030%20Biometric%20Sensing%20Mouse.htm) | **Very close** to our sensor set | **Not new** as a sensor mouse. They were built for **stress and wellbeing at work**, not for authenticating transactions or detecting coercion |
| **Duress and coercion detection** | Duress PINs and codes are standard ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duress_code), [glossary](https://nhimg.org/glossary/duress-code/)); an old patent describes biometric verification with a silent alarm on duress ([US20070198850A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20070198850A1/en), status not checked); a 2025 paper studies [coercion detection with physiological and behavioural biometrics plus an LLM](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/19/10658) | **Close** on the concept, and **very close** to "an LLM reading multimodal signals for coercion" | **Not new.** The paper's own conclusion says telling coercion from ordinary stress remains hard, which is our biggest weak spot too |
| **Hardware wallets: decoys and hidden wallets** | Duress PINs, decoy wallets and plausible deniability are common: [BlueWallet](https://bluewallet.io/docs/plausible-deniability/), [Trezor hidden wallet and passphrase](https://blog.trezor.io/passphrase-the-ultimate-protection-for-your-accounts-3a311990925b), [a 2026 review graded for coercion](https://hoge.gg/hardware-wallet-reviews-2026-wrench-attack-duress/) | **Close** on "a decoy approval under duress" | **Not new.** Ours triggers the decoy **passively** (no deliberate PIN) and can't tell the attacker anything |
| **Post-quantum signing hardware** | NIST standardised ML-DSA ([FIPS 204](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/204/final)); Yubico has [demoed a post-quantum signature prototype on a security key](https://www.yubico.com/blog/future-proofing-authentication-a-look-at-the-future-of-post-quantum-cryptography/); chip vendors list PQC support | **Emerging** | **Not new**, and others will ship it. Our use is standard ML-DSA-65, which is a strength (no custom crypto), not a novelty |
| **A signer hidden in an everyday peripheral** | **We found nothing** that places a transaction-signing authenticator inside a mouse. Hardware wallets resemble USB sticks, fobs and cards ([overview](https://www.trmlabs.com/guides/common-crypto-artifacts-and-their-role-in-investigations)); the closest hit is criminals [hiding password readers in USB-like devices](https://www.ft.com/content/70f69253-6017-4259-95c9-48bf0e088b40?syn-25a6b1a6=1), which is the opposite use | **None found** | This is the **most defensible gap**, but a few web searches can't prove it. A proper patent search would be needed to claim it |

## What is genuinely different (defensible, with the caveat above)

1. **No input at all, and no visible authenticator.** Mouse authentication research mostly reads cursor data in software; biometric mice need a deliberate touch. We found none that is **hidden, input-free and gates a cryptographic signature**.
2. **Hardware-attested sensing.** The STM32 MACs the sensor summary into the tag, and the verifier recomputes the owner check from it, so **endpoint malware can't replay or fabricate** the behavioural data. We didn't find mouse-dynamics work that attests its features this way.
3. **One passive stream for identity and for duress.** Research treats these separately. Ours uses the same data for "is it the owner?" and "are they acting freely?", with different quiet outcomes: an ordinary failure for another hand, a decoy for coercion.
4. **Misdirection in the logs.** An approval looks like a signature from a generic signer, so an attacker assumes a remote key. We found no equivalent design (but it is hard to search for).
5. **Zero-input approval tied to post-quantum, hash-chained, named accountability** for an institutional audience.

## What we must not claim

- **"A completely new technology" or "the first".** Each part has prior art, much of it academic or commercial.
- **That it is accurate.** The mouse-dynamics literature shows real accuracy is data-dependent, and we have **no measured error rates**. Report ours from the [owner-check test](OWNER-CHECK.md#how-we-will-find-out-if-it-works-hackathon-test).
- **That it detects coercion.** Even recent research says separating coercion from ordinary stress is unsolved. Say "an early warning", not "detects".
- **That it can't be defeated.** The honest line is in [STEALTH-APPROVAL.md](STEALTH-APPROVAL.md#what-we-lose-read-this).

## Facts used in the pitch, checked

The Binance incident (29 October 2026, about US$6.3 to 6.4 billion, hot wallets on several chains) is confirmed by [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/tether-circle-freeze-238-billion-after-binance-reports-security-incident-2026-10-29/) and [Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/insights/binance-largest-hack-in-history/). Reports, including [Thoropass's summary of Binance's report](https://www.thoropass.com/blog/binance-breach-2026), say attackers reached the signing service through a compromised developer laptop and an **internal AI tool**, and the FBI attributed it to North Korea's TraderTraitor group. That supports "the AI-accelerated world" in the pitch: the signing service accepted instructions from a path that was compromised by an AI-assisted route.
